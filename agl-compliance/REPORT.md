# AGL Energy (ASX: AGL) — announcement compliance screen

**Window:** 25 May – 25 August 2026 · **Compiled:** 25 August 2026

> **This is a first-pass risk screen, not legal advice.** It was compiled from
> secondary sources (news coverage, announcement aggregators, regulator
> publications) because the research environment could not access ASX or AGL
> primary documents directly. Every amber finding is a *question to verify
> against the lodged PDFs*, not an asserted breach. See Limitations.

## Executive summary

**No red-flag breaches were identified.** AGL's in-window market disclosure
looks orderly: FY26 results were delivered on the pre-announced date, landed
inside the guidance range last narrowed on 6 May 2026, the dividend and AGM
notices were complete and timely, and no trading halts, ASX queries, leaks, or
guidance misses surfaced. Unusually, AGL's preferred "underlying" profit
measure ($631m) was this year *lower* than statutory profit ($756m, inflated by
one-off gains), which blunts the usual non-IFRS prominence criticism.

Six **amber** items are worth verifying, in rough priority order:

1. **Silence on the 19 Aug Full Court Centrepay win.** The Full Federal Court
   quashed the $25m AER penalty (the largest ever under national energy laws)
   and the AER must repay it — a week after AGL signed FY26 accounts. No AGL
   ASX announcement was found. Probably defensible on materiality (~4% of
   underlying NPAT), but the accounting treatment and the disclosure decision
   deserve a look.
2. **Silence on the 8 Jul Loy Yang "coal shortfall" episode.** Unit trips and
   coal-conservation rebids across Loy Yang A during a >$300/MWh evening spike,
   with no market announcement, and results messaging a month later emphasising
   a "mild winter." Likely transient and immaterial — but verify duration.
3. **"On track" renewables-pipeline claims vs the wind gap.** AGL reiterated it
   is on track for 12 GW of new renewable and firming capacity by end-2035
   (6 GW by FY30) while building no new wind and facing sector-wide "wind
   drought" criticism. Forward-looking-statement (s 769C) reasonable-grounds
   question; the batteries are real and delivering, the wind leg is the risk.
4. **"Carbon Neutral" retail products after the Climate Active exit.** AGL
   withdrew from Climate Active effective 3 Feb 2026, yet carbon-neutral
   product pages remained live through the window and third-party references to
   Climate Active certification persist. If on-page claims still trade on the
   certification, that is classic ACL s 18/29 territory (compare
   EnergyAustralia's product withdrawal and apology).
5. **First mandatory AASB S2 climate statements** (in the 12 Aug Annual
   Report). Nothing adverse found — but this is AGL's highest-stakes new
   disclosure document, published into an active ASIC review cycle, and only
   the primary text can clear it.
6. **Torrens Island "low-carbon Energy Hub" framing** while the site's green
   hydrogen hub is listed as archived by CSIRO and the B station's closure has
   been pushed from 2026 to 2028 (at SA Government request, publicly
   announced). Verify what the hub claims currently promise.

On the **consumer lens**, nothing unlawful surfaced, but the optics stack is
real: record statutory profit and a raised dividend announced alongside AER
data showing AGL hardship-program participation up roughly a third
year-on-year, a cut of the standing-offer solar feed-in tariff to 0c from
1 July, and standing "loyalty tax" criticism of the sector. These are
reputational exposures that make the wording of "helping customers" claims in
investor materials worth watching.

The screen also caught and corrected **two false positives** produced by
initial search synthesis — a reminder of why amber findings here need primary
verification (see "Corrections" below).

## Per-announcement assessment

### 21 Jul — FY26 results date and webcast notice — GREEN
Routine administrative notice; the 12 Aug date had been flagged since the
"2026 Key Dates" release of 19 Jan 2026. No issues.

### 12 Aug — FY26 Results Announcement and FY27 Guidance — GREEN
The key continuous-disclosure test of the window, and AGL passes it on the
available evidence:

- **Guidance was met, so no update was owed.** FY26 delivered underlying
  EBITDA $2,100m against the 6 May range of $2,060–2,180m, and underlying NPAT
  $631m against $610–680m — inside both ranges (each below midpoint). Under
  Listing Rule 3.1 / Guidance Note 8, an update between 25 May and results day
  was only required if AGL expected a material departure from published
  guidance; landing within the range is strong ex-post evidence none was
  needed — even against the backdrop of the Q2 wholesale price collapse (AEMO
  reported NEM average spot down 47% year-on-year in July) and Macquarie's
  13 Jul downgrade. Verify by checking internal forecasts if this ever became
  contentious; on public facts, no issue.
- **Non-IFRS presentation (RG 230) is unusually low-risk this year.**
  Statutory NPAT ($756m, up $644m) exceeded underlying NPAT ($631m, down ~2%)
  because of one-offs: +$268m Tilt Renewables divestment gain, +$179m
  fair-value movements, +$67m onerous-contract revaluation. Leading with
  "underlying" therefore *understates* rather than flatters — the reverse of
  the pattern ASIC's RG 230 targets. Reuters-syndicated coverage explicitly
  carried both figures. Residual verify: statutory figures given equal
  prominence and reconciled in the release itself (AGL's stated practice
  references RG 230).
- **FY27 guidance is candid.** Ranges (EBITDA $1,900–2,200m, NPAT $470–670m)
  have midpoints *below* FY26 actuals, with stated drivers (lower wholesale
  prices rolling through contracted positions, legacy gas contracts rolling
  off). A softer outlook honestly given is the opposite of a misleading one.
- **Minor tone note (no rating impact):** "Our strong full-year results
  reflected excellent business performance" sits alongside underlying NPAT
  down ~2% and a softer FY27 outlook; the release itself attributes the
  headline statutory jump to asset sales. Puffery within normal bounds.

### 12 Aug — FY26 Results Presentation — AMBER
Two forward-looking claim clusters warrant a reasonable-grounds check
(Corporations Act s 769C; ASX GN 8 expectations for forward-looking
statements):

- **"On track" transition pipeline claims.** The deck reiterates the CTAP
  trajectory — 12 GW of new renewable and firming capacity by end-2035, 6 GW
  by FY30 (≥3 GW batteries), flexible fleet now 8.7 GW. The battery leg is
  demonstrably delivering (Liddell 500 MW operational in the window; Tomago
  500 MW/2,000 MWh in construction; ~20% claimed yield on battery capex). The
  wind leg is not: AGL is building no new wind, its named wind projects
  (Hexham, Barn Hill, Pottinger) are pre-construction, and in-window sector
  coverage (ABC, 25 Aug; RenewEconomy) describes a wind "drought" with
  gentailers criticised for not building what they can't control. **Verify:**
  whether the presentation's "on track" language is tied to disclosed
  assumptions and staging, or asserts progress the wind pipeline can't yet
  support. An unqualified "on track for 12 GW" is the kind of claim ASIC's
  greenwashing programme (INFO 271; four civil penalties to date, most
  recently Fiducian's $7.3m) tests for reasonable grounds.
- **"Batteries as the new cash cow" earnings framing.** Battery fleet EBITDA
  was $57m (+$10m) in FY26 — real but small against $2.1bn group EBITDA, and
  AEMO data shows battery price spreads down 85% year-on-year. Check the deck
  doesn't extrapolate FY26 battery economics into headline growth claims
  without the volatility caveat AGL itself applies elsewhere.

### 12 Aug — Appendix 4E and Annual Report 2026 — AMBER
Amber is verification-driven, not suspicion-driven; three primary-source
checks matter:

- **First mandatory climate statements (Corporations Act s 296A / AASB S2).**
  AGL states the Sustainability Report section of the Annual Report contains
  the required climate-related financial disclosures with assurance
  statements. This is AGL's first year in the mandatory regime, published
  into an active ASIC review (ASIC's May 2026 "early observations" praised
  first-wave quality but found only ~30% exceeded minimum assurance scope;
  final observations due 2H 2026). Check: scenario analysis choices, scope 3
  treatment under first-year relief, consistency between CTAP targets and the
  audited statements, and consistency of any consumer-facing green claims
  with these disclosures. Nothing adverse was found in coverage — but nobody
  independent appears to have read it closely yet.
- **PPA reclassification restatement carry-through.** At the half-year AGL
  restated the classification of a number of renewable power purchase
  agreements (and dual-fuel volume conventions). Confirm the FY26 accounts
  disclose the restatement transparently and prior-period comparability is
  properly explained (s 1041H risk lives in quiet restatements).
- **OFR balance (s 299A / RG 247).** The results narrative leans on
  transition upside; confirm the OFR gives equal treatment to the disclosed
  downside drivers — wholesale price weakness (structural, per the Macquarie
  "oversupplied to FY31" thesis), gas cost roll-off, and coal closure
  execution risk.

### 12 Aug — Dividend notice — GREEN
Final 26c fully franked; ex-date 25 Aug, record 26 Aug, payment 24 Sep; FY26
total 50c; payout 53.3% of underlying NPAT, within the stated policy. Dates
internally consistent and consistent with coverage. (One aggregator's
"interim" label and another's 25c figure are their errors, corroborated
nowhere else.) No issues.

### 14 Aug — Notice of 2026 AGM — GREEN
Lodged 48 days before the 1 Oct AGM (28 days required, s 249HA). Business is
standard (remuneration report, three re-elections including the Chair,
243,623 performance rights to the CEO under the approved LTIP, termination
benefits, proportional takeover renewal). No proxy controversy found; the
2025 AGM passed all resolutions with no strike. No issues.

### Aug — Appendix 3Y notices (Nicks; Donaghey) — GREEN (provisional)
Both trades occurred post-results at ~$8.80 — i.e. outside the blackout
window, which is good practice, and the Donaghey notice reportedly states the
trade was outside a closed period. Two verifications remain: (a) lodgement
within 5 business days (dates could not be pinned from coverage); (b) that
these are the *only* in-window 3Ys — the register here is likely incomplete
for routine notices.

## Cross-cutting findings

### C1 — No announcement of the Full Court Centrepay win (19 Aug) — AMBER
The Full Federal Court upheld AGL's appeal against the December 2024 findings
and $25m penalty over Centrepay overcharging (holding that passively retained
overpayments were not an "overcharge" under r 31 NERR); the AER must repay the
$25m AGL paid in January 2025 and issued a same-day public statement. No AGL
ASX announcement was found.

- *For AGL:* ~4% of underlying NPAT is likely below the materiality line; the
  information was immediately public via the court and the AER, so investors
  were not uninformed; the share price reaction (none reported) supports
  immateriality.
- *Worth checking anyway:* (a) whether the FY26 financial statements — signed
  a week before judgment — carried the matter as a contingency, and whether a
  subsequent-events disclosure obligation arose for the $25m recovery;
  (b) AGL's own precedent — it publicised the penalty context in 2024 with an
  apology; symmetry of disclosure is a fairness-optics point even where
  LR 3.1 does not bite; (c) whether the AER seeks High Court special leave
  (a live contingency for FY27 reporting).

### C2 — No announcement of the 8 Jul Loy Yang episode — AMBER (low)
On 8 July all six Loy Yang units (AGL's A station plus Alinta's B) rebid
capacity citing coal shortfall/conservation; A2 tripped and A4 tripped three
times returning from forced outage; evening NEM prices exceeded $300/MWh.
Single-outlet sourcing (WattClarity, two articles). Individual unit trips are
routine and not disclosable; a *sustained* fuel-supply constraint at AGL's
largest asset would be different. FY26 delivered within guidance, which
argues the episode was transient. **Verify:** duration of the coal
conservation posture and whether it persisted into FY27 (relevant to FY27
guidance assumptions), and AEMO records for the period.

### C3 — Consumer-lens optics stack — OBSERVATION (no breach identified)
Not a listing-rule matter, but the pattern regulators and media reward with
scrutiny:

- Record statutory profit ($756m) and a raised dividend announced 12 Aug,
  while AER quarterly data showed >110,000 AGL customers in arrears at
  end-March 2026 and hardship-program participation up roughly a third
  year-on-year (21,456 → 28,168) — the advocacy framing ("record profits
  while customers drown in debt", Parents for Climate) is single-source but
  built on AER data.
- Standing-offer solar feed-in tariff cut to 0c/kWh from 1 Jul 2026, drawing
  "donating power to the retailer" criticism, while retail rates on the same
  contracts sit at 29–37c/kWh.
- Sector-wide "loyalty tax" criticism (CHOICE) of pricing structures as DMO
  offers fell for most customers on 1 Jul (SA the exception).

None of this evidences unlawful conduct by AGL in the window — the Full Court
in fact *cleared* AGL's most prominent consumer matter. The exposure is the
gap between "helping customers with cost of living" positioning and this
data; wording of consumer claims in investor materials and marketing should
be tested against it. Historic ESC penalties (best-offer miscalculation,
price-certainty breaches, wrongful disconnection — ~$1.8m across 2023–25) show
the conduct baseline regulators will measure against.

### C4 — Carbon Neutral products after the Climate Active exit — AMBER
AGL withdrew from the Climate Active certification scheme effective
3 February 2026 (ahead of the scheme's announced wind-up — a July 2026
consultation confirms certification ends 30 June 2027). Yet AGL "Carbon
Neutral" residential and business product pages remained live through the
window, and third-party comparison content still describes AGL's offsets as
"certified by Climate Active". A signed Climate Active disclosure statement
for AGL's CY2024 gas product was uploaded as late as June 2026 (legacy
certification). **Verify against the live pages:** whether any current AGL
marketing still claims Climate Active certification post-exit, and what
substantiates "carbon neutral" now that the certification is gone. The
EnergyAustralia precedent (products withdrawn, customer apology, after legal
challenge) shows where this goes if the claims outlive their basis. This is
the screen's clearest potential ACL s 18/29 exposure — currently unverified
because the live pages could not be fetched.

### C5 — Torrens Island "Energy Hub" framing — AMBER (low)
The 2022 closure announcement promised transformation "into a low-carbon
industrial Energy Hub", with hydrogen flagged as "potential". Since then: the
B station's closure moved from 30 Jun 2026 to 1 Jul 2028 at the SA
Government's (paid) request — publicly announced Oct 2025, so no disclosure
issue — and CSIRO's HyResource lists the Torrens Island Green Hydrogen Hub as
archived. The battery (250 MW, operating) and Barker Inlet investment are
real. **Verify:** whether current hub marketing still implies hydrogen/green
industrial outcomes that are no longer active, and whether "low-carbon"
fairly describes a site now running gas two years longer than the claim's
original context.

### C6 — Coal closure commitments — GREEN
In-window, the CEO reaffirmed "no plans to change our closure dates"
(Bayswater 2033, Loy Yang A end-FY35). The Loy Yang date is contractually
underpinned by the Structured Transition Agreement with Victoria, and the
scope 3 target (−60% by FY35) is anchored to those closures. Mothballing/
two-shifting commentary is operationally consistent with, not contrary to,
the dates. NSW ministerial pressure to extend coal is a known risk but there
is no evidence AGL's statements lack reasonable grounds. The Torrens B
extension shows closure dates can move — each future reaffirmation should
keep carrying that qualification.

### C7 — Transaction-completion silence (Tilt, telco) — GREEN
Completion of two previously announced divestments fell in the window with no
AGL-ticker announcement found: Tilt Renewables proceeds ($739m net) received
in May, and the telco sale completed 15 Jun (announced by Aussie Broadband;
AGL Sales lodged as a ~7.5% ABB substantial holder on ABB's ticker; the stake
is locked up). Both transactions were fully announced at signing and their
completions were on announced terms and immaterial as increments; both are
disclosed in the FY26 results. No LR 3.1 issue apparent.

## Corrections — false positives caught during the screen

Two claims produced by initial search synthesis were disproved on deeper
sourcing; they are recorded here because they would otherwise have been
headline "in-window announcements":

1. **"AGL appointed Gary Brown permanent CFO, effective immediately" is a
   19 January 2023 announcement**, not a 2026 one. Brown had been interim CFO
   since October 2022 and simply presented FY26 results as the incumbent. No
   CFO announcement exists in the window.
2. **Torrens Island B did not close on 30 June 2026.** The 2022 closure
   announcement was superseded in October 2025 when AGL and the SA Government
   agreed a funded extension of three units to 1 July 2028.

Both errors came from search-result synthesis blending old announcements into
current-quarter framing — precisely the failure mode primary-source scanning
(see `scanner/`) eliminates.

## Limitations

  asx.com.au, agl.com.au and all announcement aggregators; everything above

  carried by the cited coverage.

  holder notices, employee-equity forms, CGS/4G).
- **Search budget exhausted** before final verification passes (AGL churn
  numbers; whether AGL announced the Centrepay judgment; Loy Yang episode
  duration; live Carbon Neutral page wording; several RenewEconomy article
  dates).
- **Single-source items** are flagged inline throughout the underlying
  research and treated as unverified.
- Assessments apply public-record facts to the stated frameworks at screening
  depth; they are not legal conclusions about breach.
